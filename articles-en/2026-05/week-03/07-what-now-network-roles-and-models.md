## Notes

### Why the Roles Matter

Networking has several similar-sounding titles:

- Network Architect;
- Network Engineer;
- Network Administrator.

From the outside, they may sound almost identical. In a real organization, they can represent different responsibilities.

Understanding the difference helps you:

- read job postings more accurately;
- understand who owns what in a project;
- avoid confusion in IT meetings;
- see a possible career path forward.

### Network Architect

A Network Architect owns the vision and blueprint.

They think about questions like:

- how the network should be built;
- which protocols and standards should be used;
- how buildings, branches or data centers should connect;
- where redundancy belongs;
- how the design will scale;
- which physical and logical connections are required.

The architect does not always configure every switch manually. Their main job is design.

Analogy:

```text
Building architect designs the building.
Construction team builds it.
```

### Network Engineer

A Network Engineer takes the design and makes it real.

They work on:

- configuration;
- implementation;
- testing;
- routing and switching setup;
- firewall or edge configuration;
- wireless deployment;
- validation;
- troubleshooting during rollout.

If the architect creates the blueprint, the engineer builds the working network.

This is where design meets real configs.

### Network Administrator

A Network Administrator keeps the lights on.

Their focus:

- day-to-day maintenance;
- monitoring;
- user/network support;
- routine changes;
- troubleshooting incidents;
- checking alerts;
- keeping existing infrastructure stable.

An administrator may make small changes, but the big design and major rollout work is usually already done.

Main job:

```text
Keep the network running.
```

### Overlap in Small Companies

In a large organization, the roles may be separate:

```text
Architect designs.
Engineer implements.
Administrator operates.
```

In a small shop, one person may do all of it:

```text
Design the network.
Configure the devices.
Monitor and fix it later.
```

That is normal in IT. The title does not always tell the whole story.

### Job Titles Can Be Loose

Job postings often use titles imprecisely.

For example:

```text
Network Administrator
```

may mean:

- basic monitoring role;
- engineer-level implementation;
- firewall management;
- routing and switching;
- wireless support;
- on-call troubleshooting;
- documentation and design work.

Better approach:

```text
Do not read only the title.
Read the responsibilities.
```

Responsibilities reveal the real job.

### OSI and TCP/IP Review

OSI and TCP/IP describe network communication.

TCP/IP is what actually runs in modern networks and on the internet.

OSI is the language engineers use every day:

```text
Layer 1 issue
Layer 2 problem
Check Layer 3
Transport behavior
```

For troubleshooting, OSI is especially valuable because it helps narrow the problem area quickly.

### Three-Tier Architecture Review

Three-tier architecture shows how physical and logical network design should be structured.

Layers:

- Access;
- Distribution;
- Core.

The access layer connects end devices.

The distribution layer aggregates access and provides policy/redundancy boundaries.

The core layer moves traffic between major network blocks or buildings.

This helps avoid random switch sprawl and fragile daisy chains.

## Questions

### 1. What does a Network Architect usually focus on?

Design, strategy, standards, physical/logical connections and the overall network blueprint.

### 2. What does a Network Engineer usually do?

Implements the design, configures devices, tests the network and troubleshoots during deployment.

### 3. What does a Network Administrator usually do?

Keeps the network running day to day through monitoring, maintenance and troubleshooting.

### 4. Can one person perform all three roles?

Yes. In smaller organizations, one person may act as architect, engineer and administrator.

### 5. Why should you read job responsibilities instead of only job titles?

Because companies use titles loosely, and the responsibilities show the real expected work.

### 6. Which communication model becomes daily troubleshooting language?

The OSI model.

### 7. What is TCP/IP's role?

It is the practical protocol model used by modern networks and the internet.

### 8. Why is three-tier architecture important?

It gives a structured way to design real networks with access, distribution and core layers.

### 9. What does the access layer connect?

End devices such as computers, printers, phones, APs and POS terminals.

### 10. What comes next after this foundation?

Deeper work with routing, switching, protocols, security and troubleshooting.
