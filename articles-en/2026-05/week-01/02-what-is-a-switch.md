# What is a Switch?

Source: private course page  
Date added: 2026-05-07

Tags: switch, lan, ethernet, mac address, local network

## Summary

A switch is the device that connects wired devices inside a local network. It gives computers, printers, servers, phones and other endpoints a central place to communicate.

Main idea: a switch is the foundation of most LANs. It keeps local communication organized instead of forcing every device to connect directly to every other device.

## Key Points

- A switch connects devices in a LAN.
- LAN means Local Area Network.
- Devices connect to switch ports with Ethernet cables.
- Switch ports are also called interfaces.
- Switches learn where devices live by using MAC addresses.
- Business networks use switches to scale local connectivity.
- A switch is not the same thing as a router.
- A switch handles local communication; a router connects networks.

## Notes

### Why Switches Exist

If every device had to connect directly to every other device, cabling would become impossible very quickly. A switch solves that by acting as the central connection point.

```text
PC -> Switch
Printer -> Switch
Server -> Switch
Phone -> Switch
```

### Switch as the LAN Foundation

At NetworkChuck Coffee, the switch connects local devices such as registers, office PCs, printers, phones and access points.

Wireless may look separate to users, but the access points normally plug back into the switch.

### MAC Address Learning

Switches use MAC addresses to learn which device is connected to which port. That lets the switch forward traffic more intelligently instead of sending everything everywhere.

## Questions

### What problem does a switch solve?

It gives many local devices a central place to connect and communicate.

### Does a switch connect different networks?

Not as its main job. A router is used to connect different networks.
