## Summary

Making an Ethernet cable is not something you will do every day, but it is a valuable hands-on skill. Terminating, crimping, testing and troubleshooting a cable makes Ethernet feel less mysterious.

Main idea: building one cable teaches you how twisted pairs, pinouts, connectors and testing fit together.

## Key Points

- Ethernet cable contains four twisted pairs, eight wires total.
- Twists reduce EMI and crosstalk.
- UTP means Unshielded Twisted Pair.
- STP means Shielded Twisted Pair.
- 10BASE-T and 100BASE-TX used two pairs.
- 1000BASE-T uses all four pairs.
- Straight-through cables use the same pinout on both ends.
- Crossover cables swap transmit and receive pairs.
- Modern Auto-MDIX reduces the need for crossover cables.
- T568B is a common wiring standard.
- Cable testers verify pin order and continuity.
- Copper Ethernet runs typically top out at 100 meters.

```text
White orange
Orange
White green
Blue
White blue
Green

Brown
```

### Build Process

Basic flow:

```text
Strip jacket
Untwist pairs
Arrange wires
Trim evenly
Insert into RJ45
Crimp
Repeat on other end
Test
```

## Questions

### Why do twisted pairs matter?

They reduce noise and interference.

### Why use a cable tester?

It quickly reveals wiring mistakes before troubleshooting gets painful.
