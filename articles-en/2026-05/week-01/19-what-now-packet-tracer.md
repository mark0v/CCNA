## Summary

Packet Tracer is a starting point, not the finish line. Once the basics are comfortable, the best next step is to keep building small labs, changing things, breaking them and learning from what happens.

Main idea: Packet Tracer becomes powerful when you stop only following labs and start asking, "What happens if I try this?"

## Key Points

- Packet Tracer is a safe place to test ideas.
- It is useful for certification study, job prep and curiosity.
- It does not replace every emulator or real device.
- It is excellent for building confidence.
- Growth happens as networking knowledge grows.
- VLANs, routing, DHCP, ACLs and switching labs can all build on it.
- Testing before touching production is a strong real-world habit.
- Confidence comes from repeated mental reps.

## Notes

### Keep the Momentum

Good practice pattern:

```text
Rebuild examples
Change one thing
Observe what breaks
Fix it
Repeat
```

### Why It Matters in Real Work

Production networks can be stressful. A lab lets you validate basics before changing real gear.

Even if Packet Tracer does not simulate every feature, it helps with the fundamentals where many mistakes happen.

### Is Packet Tracer the destination?

No. It is a practice tool for learning networking.

Build small labs, modify them and learn from failures.
