## Summary

Before building a network, you need to understand what a network is and what the major components do. Switches, routers, firewalls and wireless access points are not just buzzwords; they are roles in a design that supports the business.

Main idea: you cannot build confidently until you understand the parts and why they exist.

## Key Points

- A network is devices connected so they can communicate.
- The big picture matters before deep technical details.
- A switch connects local devices.
- A WAP/AP provides wireless access.
- A router moves traffic between networks.
- A firewall controls and secures traffic.
- Router and firewall roles can exist in the same physical box.
- Business networks support staff systems, guest WiFi, POS, cameras and office devices.
- Bad networks become business problems.
- Learn the roles first, then layer details on top.

## Notes

### What Is a Network?

A network is a system of connected devices that communicate.

At Castle Rysen Coffee, that could include:

- staff computers;
- POS systems;
- customer WiFi;
- cameras;
- office systems;
- routers and switches;
- access points.

### Device Roles

```text
Switch  -> local wired connections
AP/WAP  -> wireless access
Router  -> traffic between networks
Firewall -> security control
```

### Why It Matters

When a network fails, payments, orders, WiFi, cameras and employee work can all fail. Networking is not background decoration; it keeps the business alive.

## Commands / Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Switch | Connects local wired devices. |
| Router | Connects different networks. |
| Firewall | Filters and controls traffic. |
| WAP/AP | Wireless access point. |
| Architecture | High-level structure of the network. |

## Questions

### Why start with device roles?

Because those roles explain how the network supports the business.

### Are router and firewall the same?

No. They can overlap, but routing and security are different core functions.
