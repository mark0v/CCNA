# Scrum

## Summary

Scrum is an Agile framework for managing team work through roles, events, artifacts, and values. It helps teams deliver product value in small increments, get regular feedback, and improve their process after each sprint.

Scrum is most common in software development and engineering, but its ideas can also be applied to IT, marketing, operations, product management, and support teams.

## Key Points

- Agile is a philosophy and set of principles, while Scrum is a specific framework for applying Agile.
- Scrum is based on empiricism, lean thinking, and continuous improvement.
- A Scrum team usually includes a Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers.
- Work is organized through short iterations called sprints.
- The main artifacts are Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, and Increment.
- The main events are Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective.
- Scrum works best when transparency, fast feedback, and frequent value delivery matter.

## Notes

### What Is Scrum?

Scrum is an Agile project management framework. It helps a team organize work, inspect progress regularly, and improve the process continuously.

Scrum does not prescribe every small step. Instead, it defines a structure: roles, events, artifacts, and values. Inside that structure, the team decides how to do the work.

The main idea is iterative work: plan a small amount, complete it during a sprint, show the result, collect feedback, and improve the next cycle.

### Agile vs Scrum

Agile is a guiding philosophy. It describes how to think about product development: deliver value in small pieces, collaborate with customers, respond to change, and learn through feedback.

Scrum is a specific framework that helps teams apply Agile principles in everyday work.

The Agile Manifesto defines four values:

- individuals and interactions over processes and tools;
- working software over comprehensive documentation;
- customer collaboration over contract negotiation;
- responding to change over following a plan.

Scrum turns those ideas into practice through sprints, roles, events, and artifacts.

### Scrum Theory

Scrum is based on **empiricism** and **lean thinking**.

**Empiricism** means that knowledge comes from experience and decisions are made based on what is observed. Scrum constantly checks reality through inspect and adapt.

**Lean thinking** focuses on reducing waste and keeping only what creates value.

Scrum has three pillars:

- **Transparency** - work, progress, and problems are visible.
- **Inspection** - the team regularly inspects the product and process.
- **Adaptation** - the team changes the plan or process when problems appear.

### Scrum Team

A Scrum team is a small, cross-functional, self-managing team responsible for delivering a valuable product increment.

It usually has around 10 or fewer people. The team should have all skills needed to create and verify the increment.

Main roles:

- Product Owner;
- Scrum Master;
- Developers.

### Product Owner

The Product Owner is responsible for maximizing product value.

They manage the Product Backlog, set priorities, and help the team understand what matters most.

A Product Owner should:

- understand business, customer, and market needs;
- maintain the Product Backlog;
- explain backlog items to the team;
- decide priorities;
- help decide when an increment can be released.

Important: the Product Owner is one person, not a committee. The team needs one clear source of product direction.

### Scrum Master

The Scrum Master helps the team use Scrum effectively.

They are not a traditional manager. Their role is coach, facilitator, and servant leader.

The Scrum Master:

- helps the team understand the Scrum process;
- removes impediments;
- improves transparency and flow;
- facilitates Scrum events;
- helps the Product Owner and organization work better with Scrum.

### Developers

Developers are the people who create the product increment.

In Scrum, developers are not only programmers. They are all specialists needed for delivery: frontend, backend, QA, UX, DevOps, data specialists, and others.

A strong Scrum team:

- is self-organizing;
- is cross-functional;
- works with a "we" mindset;
- helps each other complete the sprint goal;
- uses past experience to improve planning.

### Scrum Artifacts

Scrum artifacts make work transparent. They show what the team plans to do, what it is working on now, and what result has been created.

#### Product Backlog

The Product Backlog is an ordered list of all work that may be needed for the product.

It may include:

- features;
- requirements;
- enhancements;
- bug fixes;
- technical work;
- research tasks.

The Product Owner is responsible for Product Backlog content, ordering, and freshness.

#### Sprint Backlog

The Sprint Backlog is the set of backlog items selected for the current sprint, plus the plan for achieving the sprint goal.

It may change during the sprint, but the sprint goal should not be lost.

#### Increment

The Increment is the usable result of a sprint.

It should meet the Definition of Done. For one team, "done" may mean "released to customers"; for another, it may mean "tested and ready for demo." The important part is that the team shares the same understanding.

### Scrum Events

Scrum events help the team keep rhythm, inspect progress regularly, and adapt.

#### Sprint

A Sprint is a fixed time-box, usually from one to four weeks.

During a sprint, the team works toward the sprint goal and creates an increment.

The sprint length should stay stable so the team can better estimate capacity and improve planning.

#### Sprint Planning

Sprint Planning is the event where the team decides:

- what sprint goal to choose;
- which backlog items to bring into the sprint;
- how the goal will be achieved.

By the end of planning, everyone should understand what will be delivered and why.

#### Daily Scrum

Daily Scrum is a short daily meeting, usually up to 15 minutes.

The goal is to inspect progress toward the sprint goal, synchronize, and identify blockers.

Classic questions:

- What did I do yesterday?
- What will I do today?
- Are there any blockers?

The meeting should not become reporting for the sake of reporting. Daily Scrum exists so the team can plan the next 24 hours.

#### Sprint Review

Sprint Review happens at the end of the sprint.

The team shows stakeholders what was completed, gets feedback, and discusses what may affect the Product Backlog.

It is not just a demo. It is a chance to inspect whether the product is moving in the right direction.

#### Sprint Retrospective

Sprint Retrospective is a meeting about the team's process.

The team discusses:

- what worked well;
- what got in the way;
- what can be improved in the next sprint.

Retrospective is essential for continuous improvement.

### Scrum Values

Scrum is based on five values:

- **Commitment** - the team makes realistic commitments and works toward a shared goal.
- **Courage** - the team is willing to discuss problems and try new approaches.
- **Focus** - the team focuses on the sprint goal.
- **Openness** - progress, blockers, and risks are discussed openly.
- **Respect** - team members respect each other's contribution, the Product Owner, the Scrum Master, and stakeholders.

### Scrum vs Kanban

Scrum uses fixed-length sprints, roles, events, and sprint goals.

Kanban focuses on continuous flow. Work items move across a board, and WIP limits help avoid overloading the team.

Scrum is better for product or feature development where structure, planning, and predictable increments are useful.

Kanban is often better for support, operations, and teams with a continuous stream of incoming tasks.

Some teams use a hybrid approach, such as Scrumban: they keep Scrum's backlog and planning discipline while adding Kanban flow and WIP limits.

### When Scrum Is Useful

Scrum is useful when:

- the product is complex and needs regular feedback;
- requirements may evolve;
- the team wants to deliver value frequently;
- stakeholders are ready to participate in reviews;
- progress transparency is important;
- the team is willing to improve its process regularly.

### Common Scrum Problems

Even experienced teams can face problems:

- roles are unclear;
- the Product Owner is unavailable;
- the team overcommits;
- ceremonies are done formally but without purpose;
- Daily Scrum becomes a status report;
- retrospective does not lead to action;
- Definition of Done is unclear.

Scrum works not because of meeting names, but because of transparency, feedback, and willingness to improve the process.

## Commands / Terms

- **Scrum** - an Agile framework for iterative product work.
- **Sprint** - a fixed time-box for creating an increment.
- **Product Owner** - the role responsible for product value and the Product Backlog.
- **Scrum Master** - the role helping the team apply Scrum.
- **Developers** - specialists who create the product increment.
- **Product Backlog** - an ordered list of potential product work.
- **Sprint Backlog** - selected work for the current sprint and the plan for doing it.
- **Increment** - the usable result of a sprint.
- **Definition of Done** - shared criteria for completed work.
- **Daily Scrum** - daily team synchronization.
- **Sprint Review** - inspection of the sprint result.
- **Sprint Retrospective** - inspection of the team's process.

## Questions

**1. What is the difference between Agile and Scrum?**  
Agile is a philosophy and set of principles. Scrum is a specific framework for applying Agile in team work.

**2. What are the three main Scrum roles?**  
Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers.

**3. What are the three Scrum artifacts?**  
Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, and Increment.

**4. What is Daily Scrum for?**  
It helps the team inspect progress toward the sprint goal, synchronize, and identify blockers.

**5. How is Sprint Review different from Retrospective?**  
Sprint Review inspects the product increment and collects feedback. Retrospective inspects the team's process and looks for improvements.

**6. When can Scrum work poorly?**  
When roles are unclear, requirements are chaotic, stakeholders do not provide feedback, the team does not improve its process, or ceremonies are only formal.
